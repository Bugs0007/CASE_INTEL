[span] Sign-in
[h3] One account, one advocate.
[p] Sign up with Google, or from an invitation link with a username and password. Google sends Case Intel your name and verified email address, never your Google password.
[span] Per-advocate isolation
[h3] Every record has an owner.
[p] Cases, documents, hearings, fees: each row carries its owner, and every list, view and edit is filtered to the advocate signed in. A test fails if a new endpoint is added without being scoped or deliberately reviewed.
[span] Owner-scoped retrieval
[h3] Case Bot only searches your documents.
[p] The search takes the owner as a required input and filters every leg by it, meaning and keyword alike. Narrowing to one case narrows within your documents; it never replaces the owner filter.
[h3] What it won't do
[li] It won't read cause lists for seven High Courts (Allahabad, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Punjab & Haryana) or the Madurai bench of Madras. The eCourts portal serves no usable list for them, so those matters say “Not covered” rather than “Not listed.”
[li] It doesn't track Supreme Court cases.
[li] It won't tell two advocates with the same name apart. It shows the court's own record and lets you judge.
[li] It won't hard-block a conflict. It warns; you decide.
[li] It won't apply limitation exceptions for you. Sections 12(2), 4 and 5 stay as notes.
[li] It won't send routine adjournment orders to the AI at all.
[li] It won't file anything on your behalf.
[li] It isn't affiliated with eCourts or with any court.
